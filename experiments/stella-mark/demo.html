A star with a face

Stella's six-ray brand star, rigged the way Grok Bot rigs its blob: one render, one rAF loop, springs at a fixed substep, and the character itself becoming the middle dot when it thinks. Standalone — nothing here is wired into the app.

Try thinking. The star balls up, drops its face, and joins a three-slot bounce as the middle dot. Then working: the star stays a star and a light wave runs around its rays. Then loading, where a band of compression travels up the body and the face squeezes along with it.

Every state

Sixteen states, each with its own eye-pose pool, blink cadence and face tuning. Seven of them add an overlay; the rest just change expression.

At the sizes it has to work

The rig measures its own rendered width every 500 ms. Below 44 px the thinking dots zoom 1.5×, above 134 px they don't — so one component covers a 16 px sidebar chip and a 200 px empty state.

Where the shape comes from

One source of truth: the six-ray star already shipping in WorkingStar.tsx. But the logo is not a tidy six. Its vertical pair runs to full length while the four side rays reach barely half, and those four sit in two close pairs about 37° apart rather than evenly around the circle. Gorgeous as a flare at 512 px. Shrink it to 24 px and put a face in it and the long pair dominates while the close pairs merge into horizontal arms — it reads as a kite, not a star.

So the character profile is rebuilt from the logo's own ray set, one correction at a time:

morph puff 1.00 logo → character

puff crossfades the rebuilt profile against the untouched logo, which is why the rig can morph between the character and the brand mark instead of swapping one asset for another.

Using it

import { createStellaMark } from "./stella-mark.js";

const mark = createStellaMark(el, {
  size: 28,
  state: "idle",
  shape: "star",          // star | pebble | brand | orb
  ink: "aurora",          // the shipping WorkingStar ramp
  followPointer: true,
});

mark.setState("thinking");
mark.setShape("brand");     // morphs, does not swap
mark.sparkle(18);           // one-off burst
mark.destroy();

What it inherits from the Grok rig

  • One render, one loop. A fixed node pool — 2 dots, 3 dot-sparkles, 5 orbit sparkles, 4 rings, 2 eyes — created once and then shown, hidden and re-transformed by hand.
  • Springs at a fixed 1/120 s substep, dt clamped to 0.1 s, so a slow frame doesn't launch the star off screen.
  • It parks itself. When every spring has settled and no particles are alive, the loop stops instead of burning a frame to redraw the same picture.
  • Ring-lerp morphing. Every outline is 96 points on 96 rays from the centre, so any shape lerps into any other and re-emits as one Catmull-Rom path.
  • The three-slot Gaussian bounce, 1400 ms, σ 0.15, with the body as slot 1 — the character does not sit beside the dots, it becomes the middle one.
  • Reduced motion is read once at mount and short-circuits the physics rather than being a class you can forget to apply.

What is Stella's

  • The star stays a star. Grok collapses to a dot for all fourteen of its activities. Here only thinking does — a star has rays worth animating, so working runs a light wave around them instead.
  • The silhouette is derived, not drawn. Four knobs turn the shipping logo into a character and back, so there is no second asset to keep in sync when the brand mark changes.
  • squeeze instead of a spin. A Y-axis flip is the obvious motion for a mark and the wrong one here: a flip only reads on a shape whose silhouette is roughly the same at every angle, and a six-ray star at 40° is a different star while at 90° it is a stick. So loading runs a band of compression up the body instead — the waist pinches, the material ahead of it swells and rides upward, and the whole star stretches a little as the band crosses the middle. It deforms the silhouette on purpose and never stops being a star.
  • Star eyes for celebrate, and an ambient glow that swells with the ray wave.
  • Idle starlight. A 1.2 % three-lobe swell drifting around the rays — below the threshold of "animation", above the threshold of "alive".
